## One. No double bogeys.

### Why

Doubles come from playing for birdie where the hole was asking for par or bogey,
so the count reads your strategy rather than your ball striking. In strokes
gained a double is six times worse than a bogey.

### What to do

Manage the risk instead of forcing the birdie. Avoid undue risk and manage what
you expect from a hole.

## Two. No bogeys on par 5s.

### Why

Aggression dressed up as opportunity. It usually happens after a bad tee shot or
a bad layup on a hole you had already called a birdie hole, and the mistake is
trying to force your way back into position.

### What to do

Get as close to the green as you can without taking on a hazard. Nothing about a
par five is worth bringing a penalty stroke into play.

## Three. No three-putts.

### Why

Speed, not read. The ones that hurt come from 20 to 40 feet, trying to make the
first one and running it five feet past.

### What to do

Practice speed uphill and downhill at varying distances. Half your putting time
on speed, a quarter on five to fifteen footers, a quarter simulating a putting
course.

## Four. No bogeys with nine iron or less (about 150 yards and in).

### Why

Bogeys from your scoring clubs mean the target was wrong, not the swing.

### What to do

Stop being overly aggressive and stop trying to force birdies. From 79 to 76, 82
percent of the improvement is bogeys avoided rather than birdies made.

## Five. No double chips.

### Why

A blown easy save is forcing something the spot did not allow. If you are
already in trouble, the bogey is the good outcome.

### What to do

Aim for a one to one ratio. When you are further from the green than the pin is,
get the ball on the putting surface safely instead of playing the risky shot.

## Four or fewer.

Not perfect golf, fewer disasters.
