One. No double bogeys.

Doubles come from playing for birdie where the hole was asking for par or bogey,
so the count reads your strategy rather than your ball striking. In strokes
gained a double is six times worse than a bogey. Manage the risk instead of
forcing the birdie.

Two. No bogeys on par 5s.

This one catches aggression dressed up as opportunity, usually after a bad tee
shot or a bad layup on a hole you had already called a birdie hole. Get as close
to the green as you can without taking on a hazard.

Three. No three-putts.

Speed, not read. The ones that hurt come from 20 to 40 feet, trying to make the
first one and running it five feet past. Practice speed uphill and downhill at
varying distances: half your putting time on speed, a quarter on five to fifteen
footers, a quarter simulating a putting course.

Four. No bogeys with nine iron or less (about 150 yards and in).

Bogeys from your scoring clubs mean the target was wrong, not the swing. From 79
to 76, 82 percent of the improvement is bogeys avoided rather than birdies made.

Five. No double chips.

A blown easy save is forcing something the spot did not allow. Aim for a one to
one ratio: when you are further from the green than the pin is, get it on the
surface safely.

Four or fewer. Not perfect golf, fewer disasters.
