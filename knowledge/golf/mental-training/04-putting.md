# Session 4 - Putting: Lag It Close, Bury the Short Ones

Run time about 10 minutes. Goal: two skills, one session. First, lag putting from 20 to 60 feet on different slopes and speeds so three-putt never enters your mind. Second, ruthless conviction inside four feet.

## Lag putting - the ghost circle

Stand on a big Outlaw green. Before every lag putt, draw a ghost circle around the hole - three feet in every direction. Your job is not to make it. Your job is to die the ball inside the circle. Pace first, line second.

Twenty-five feet, flat, medium speed. Read it from the low side. See the entire roll - the first foot off the face, the skid, the true roll, the last turn as it dies into the circle. Feel the stroke length that matches. Routine. Roll it. Hear the hollow click off the face, watch the ball die on the front lip. Inside the circle, inches from falling.

> Pause.

Forty feet, uphill. The green is slower than it looks going up the hill. Feel the longer, freer stroke - hit it firm and let the hill be your brake. Ball works up the slope, slows, settles two feet past. Tap-in.

Sixty feet with a big swing of break. This is two putts in one: pick the spot where the ball turns - the apex - and lag to the apex with the right pace. Watch the whole journey: out to the high side, the turn, the slow curl down the slope, dying into the circle. Feel how soft your hands stay through the long stroke.

> Pause.

Thirty feet, downhill, slick. The scary one. Pick a spot halfway to the hole and putt to that spot - the slope does the rest. Feel the tiny stroke, the featherweight hit. Ball trickles, gathers, keeps rolling, and just reaches the circle. Exhale. That putt cannot hurt you anymore.

## Inside four feet - conviction

Now the short ones. The routine compresses here: read, one rehearsal stroke, one look, roll it. No standing over it. Speed of decision is your friend inside four feet.

Three feet, straight, uphill. The one you always want. Firm pace, back of the cup. See the line like a chalk stripe. Hear the best sound in golf - the rattle of the ball in the cup. Make it five times in a row, and hear that rattle every time.

> Pause. Five reps. Do not rush the sound.

Four feet, left edge, sliding. Pick the exact blade of grass on the lip where the ball enters. Commit to the edge - a putt aimed at the edge and struck with belief beats a perfect read with a scared stroke. Rattle.

Three and a half feet, downhill, fast. Dead-weight it - see the ball topple over the front edge with its last rotation. Soft hands, short stroke, total commitment to soft. It falls.

Last one: the four-footer that matters - eighteenth green, playing partners watching, this putt for the round. Heart rate up. Good. Routine: read, rehearsal, one look, roll. Rattle. Hold that sound as the session ends.
