Read this one. It is not a track, and there is nothing to run.

A scoring discipline, not a swing thought. If you feel like you should have shot
lower than you did, these are the five reasons you did not. None of them is about
making birdies.

Tiger's goal was six or fewer of these per tournament. Yours is four or fewer per
round.

One. No double bogeys.

Why. Doubles come from playing for birdie in places that were asking for par or
bogey. The count is a read on strategy and course management, not on ball
striking.

What to do. Aim at eliminating doubles rather than forcing birdies. Avoid undue
risk and manage what you expect from a hole. In strokes gained terms a double is
six times worse than a bogey.

Two. No bogeys on par 5s.

Why. This one catches aggression dressed up as opportunity. It usually happens
after a bad tee shot or a bad layup on a hole you had already decided was a
birdie hole, and the mistake is trying to force your way back into position.

What to do. Get as close to the green as you can without taking on a hazard.
Nothing about a par five is worth bringing a penalty stroke into play.

Three. No three-putts.

Why. Three-putts are usually a speed problem, not a read problem. The ones that
hurt come from 20 to 40 feet, trying to make the first one and running it five
feet past.

What to do. Practice speed, specifically uphill and downhill at varying
distances. Half your putting time on speed, a quarter on five to fifteen footers,
a quarter simulating a putting course.

Four. No bogeys with nine iron or less (about 150 yards and in).

Why. Bogeys from your scoring clubs mean you are aiming at the wrong targets and
putting yourself in tough spots from distances that should be routine.

What to do. Stop being overly aggressive and stop trying to force birdies. When a
scoring average comes down from 79 to 76, 82 percent of the improvement is
bogeys avoided rather than birdies made.

Five. No double chips.

Why. A blown easy save is you trying to force something from a spot that did not
allow it. If you are already in trouble, the bogey is the good outcome.

What to do. Aim for a one to one ratio: when you are further from the green than
the pin is, get the ball on the putting surface safely instead of playing the
risky shot.

Four or fewer. Not perfect golf, fewer disasters.
