# Before any of it

Read this. It is not a track.

Six sessions, written from applied sport psychology. Each one owns a single mental
skill: the pre-shot routine and commitment, arousal regulation, attentional control,
recovery after a bad shot, taking a call and committing to it, and playing under
pressure. Each session does the same three things — names a pattern you will
recognize from your own rounds, explains the mechanism behind it, and gives you a
tool you can carry to the course. Then it has you rehearse the tool in your head, because
a tool you have only heard about is not yet a tool.

The sessions own the mind, and only the mind. Clubs, targets, sides, reads and
strategy live in the course walkthroughs — three courses, eighteen holes each, and
they hold all the numbers. A session never names a hole. When one needs a shot, it
asks you to bring up a shot you know well, because the rehearsal works on the state
you build, not the hole you picture.

When to run them. Anywhere the audio can have your attention — walking, on a run,
warming up, driving, or somewhere quiet where your eyes can close. The rehearsals
cut deepest when you are still, so give each session at least one quiet listen;
after that, take it wherever you are. One session at a time. Session 1 until the
routine holds up on the course — not until it merely feels smooth. A routine
consolidates on the range and in play over weeks; these reps accelerate that, they
do not replace it. After that, run whichever session matches what went wrong the
last time you played. Session 2 before you play. Session 6 before anything that
counts.

In the car, listen to the walkthrough for wherever you are playing. The two belong
together: the walkthrough tells you what to do on every hole, and these six train
the part of you that has to do it.
