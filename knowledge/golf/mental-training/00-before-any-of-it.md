# Before any of it

Read this. It is not a track.

Six sessions. Each one owns a skill, not a shot. Clubs, targets, sides, reads and
strategy belong to the course walkthroughs — three courses, eighteen holes each, and
they hold the numbers. These six own the mind. Where a session needs a shot, it names
the course and the hole and takes that hole's call as written. It never invents one.

The holes are given to you. The state is built by you. Tempo, hands, breath, heart
rate — nobody can hand you those, and they are the whole point of a closed-eye rep.

When to run them. Evening, lights low, eyes closed, one session. Session 1 until the
routine holds up on the course, not until it feels smooth in bed — a routine
consolidates on the range and in play over weeks, and these reps accelerate that, they
do not replace it. Then run whichever session matches what went wrong last time you
played. Session 2 the night before you play. Session 6 the night before anything that
counts.

In the car, listen to the course walkthrough for wherever you are playing. Take the
first two beats and the call, and skip the rehearsal — that one is eyes closed and it
is not for driving.

There is no commute session, no putting session and no wedge session. Putting and
wedges are shot families, and the walkthroughs cover them hole by hole with real
numbers. What was worth keeping from those two files lives inside these six.
