# Session 3 - Wedges Inside 50 Yards

Run time about 10 minutes. Goal: rehearse the exact scoring shots that decide your rounds - clean lies, rough lies, short-sided and long-sided, to front, middle, and back hole locations. This shot family responds to mental reps faster than almost anything else in the game.

## The template for every wedge rep

Every wedge shot in this session runs the same way: read the lie first, because the lie writes the rules. Then pick a landing spot - a patch of green the size of a towel - not the flag. See the ball land on the towel and behave: check, release, or stop. Feel the length of swing that produces that flight. Then routine, strike, accept.

## Fairway lie, 40 yards, middle pin

Tight fairway lie on the Outlaw's zoysia. Ball sitting clean. Forty yards, plenty of green, middle hole location. Pick your towel: a spot a few paces short of the hole. See the flight - medium height, lands soft, one hop, trickles to tap-in range. Feel the clock of the swing, the crisp thump of wedge on tight turf. Run the routine. Strike it. Hear the contact, watch it finish inside three feet.

> Pause. One more rep, this time to a back pin - same swing, towel moved deeper, ball releasing to the hole.

## Rough lie, 30 yards, front pin

Now the ball is down in bluegrass rough on the Posse. You can see grass will grab the hosel and kill the spin. So the plan changes: land it earlier, expect release. Towel is on the front fringe. Feel the firmer grip, the slightly steeper strike, the committed acceleration through the grass - tentative is the only bad swing from here. Ball comes out lower, no spin, lands on the fringe, runs out to eight feet. That is a win from this lie. Accept it.

> Pause. Rep it again and hole the eight-footer to close the loop.

## Short-sided, 20 yards, tucked pin

Short-sided now. The pin is cut close to your side of the green, a bunker between you and the hole, very little green to work with. First decision: twelve feet past the hole is a good shot here. Say that in your head - twelve feet is a win. See the high, soft flight carrying the bunker, landing on your small towel, stopping fast. Feel the open face, the wide soft arms, the full commitment to loft. Strike. Ball climbs, lands quiet, settles ten feet past. Two putts here is fine; one putt is a steal. Either way your card is protected.

> Pause. One rep where the nerves show up - stomach tightens looking at the bunker. Run the routine anyway. The routine beats the nerves.

## Long-sided, 50 yards, use the whole green

Last look: long-sided, fifty yards, the hole cut on the far edge with the entire green in front of you. This is the green-light wedge. Land it early, on the front third, and let the Outlaw's big green feed it back. See the lower, driving pitch, two hops, long release, curling toward the cup. Feel the compressed strike. Watch it chase to four feet. Tap in.

> Pause. Final rep: your choice of lie and pin. Make it the wedge shot that has cost you strokes this season, and stripe it.
